## Geometry (what "bend" means)

Signed curvature `κ` (blocks⁻¹), bend radius `R = 1/κ`. The bend happens in the inner X–Y plane about
an axis parallel to inner Z. Positive `κ` turns the +Y end of the axis toward **+X** (centre of
curvature at straight-space `(R, 0, z)`); negative `κ` turns it toward −X.

Straight `(x, y, z)` → bent `(X, Y, Z)` (the coordinates the existing affine map already produces):

```
θ = κ·y
X = R − (R − x)·cos θ
Y = (R − x)·sin θ
Z = z
```

- `y` stays **arc length along the centreline**, so `innerY` still spans `[−height/2, height/2)` and the
  total turn is `height·κ` radians. The cross-section `(x, z)` is undistorted.
- `κ → 0` reduces to identity; `κ == 0` takes the current affine fast path, unchanged.
- Inverse (per block): `ρ = sqrt((X−R)² + Y²)`, `x = R − sign(R)·ρ`,
  `θ = atan2(sign(R)·Y, sign(R)·(R−X))` measured relative to the sector mid-angle so the `atan2`
  branch cut lies opposite the tube, `y = θ/κ`, `z = Z`.
- Validity, checked in the constructor (throw `IllegalArgumentException`):
  `|κ|·max(|xMin|, |xMax|) < 1` (bend radius must exceed the half width, or the inner wall folds
  through the centre) and `|κ|·(yMax − yMin) ≤ 2π` (more than one turn would self-overlap and make
  the inverse ambiguous).

In bent space the box becomes a **torus sector**: an annular sector in the XY plane
(radii `|R| − max x … |R| − min x`, angles `κ·yMin … κ·yMax`) extruded over `Z ∈ [zMin, zMax)`.

row: `P(k) = rowStart + k·step` in *bent* space). Only `computeRow()` and the per-block emission change
when a bend is present:

1. **Z slab** – exactly as today (one `k` interval).
2. **Annulus** – `ρ(k)² = (X(k)−R)² + Y(k)²` is a quadratic `A·k² + B·k + C` (`A = stepX² + stepY²`).

### Claude:

Curvature is the reciprocal of a radius measured in blocks, so for anything block-sized it is always a small fraction. The easiest way to read it is as **radians turned per block of tube length**. A curvature of 0.05 means the centre line turns 0.05 radians, about 3 degrees, every block it advances. Over a 60-block tube that adds up to 3 radians, roughly a half turn.

Two limits bound the value, and both are easier to feel as radii:

- **Lower bound on the bend radius, set by the tube width.** The bend radius `1 / curvature` must be larger than the distance from the inner Y axis to the box face it bends toward, which is `diameter / 2` for a centred space. So `|curvature| < 2 / diameter`. For a 12-block penne that is 0.167. Anything above throws. In practice the inner wall looks crushed well before that, so stay near `1 / diameter` or below. That keeps the bend radius at or above one full diameter.
- **Upper bound on the total turn, set by the length.** The tube turns `height × curvature` radians in total and may not exceed one full circle, so `|curvature| <= 2π / height`. For a 60-block tube that is 0.105.

The usable range is the overlap of those two, which is why the values that worked for you were fractions in the few-hundredths range. A quick way to pick one is to think in the quantities you already have and divide at the end:

| You want | Set curvature to | Example (diameter 12, height 60) |
|---|---|---|
| A specific total turn | `turnRadians / height` | half turn: π / 60 ≈ 0.052 |
| A specific bend radius | `1 / bendRadius` | radius 20 blocks: 0.05 |
| The tightest sane elbow | about `1 / diameter` | ≈ 0.083, which turns 60 × 0.083 ≈ 5 radians, so shorten the tube or lower it |

For Macaroni, "turn angle divided by height" is the natural knob because it fixes the look regardless of tube length.
