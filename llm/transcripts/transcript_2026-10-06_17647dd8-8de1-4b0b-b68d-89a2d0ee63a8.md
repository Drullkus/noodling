**The algorithm, and how it generalizes Bresenham**

Bresenham steps the major axis one integer at a time and tracks the minor axis incrementally. The 3D volume version does the same with the roles made explicit. The map between spaces is affine: a world block's centre pulled back through the inverse matrix gives its unique inner coordinate. Because the map is affine, moving one block along any world axis changes the inner coordinates by a constant vector, namely a column of the world-to-inner matrix. That constant delta is the DDA increment.

Traversal works as scan conversion rather than forward mapping. Forward-mapping an inner lattice through a rotation would produce duplicates and holes in the world lattice. Instead:

1. Transform the eight inner corners to world space and take the bounding box, widened to whole blocks.
2. Pick the world axis along which the box is longest as the scan axis. The other two are looped over the bounding box as rows.
3. For each row, the inner coordinates along the scan axis are `rowStart + k * step`. The six inner bounds each give a one-sided linear inequality on `k`, so the first and last block inside the cuboid are solved in closed form. This is the slab test used for ray-box intersection. No block outside the cuboid is visited on the scan axis, and empty rows cost six divides.
4. Each emitted block gets its inner coordinates from the row start plus `k` times the step, so there is no per-block matrix multiply and no accumulated drift across rows.

Choosing the longest axis matters for noodles. A thin segment lying diagonally has a bounding box far larger than the cuboid, and scanning along its length keeps the row count to the cross-section's footprint.

Bounds are half-open, `[min, max)` per inner axis, and a block belongs to the space when its centre maps inside. This gives exact block counts for axis-aligned cases and avoids double-counting at shared faces between adjacent spaces.

**Factories**

- `centered(worldCenter, rotation, sx, sy, sz)`: inner coordinates run over `[-s/2, s/2)`, so a tube is `hypot(innerX, innerY)` and a spiral is `atan2` around inner Z.
- `cornered(worldOrigin, rotation, sx, sy, sz)`: inner coordinates run over `[0, s)`.
- `segment(from, to, width, height)`: the direct volumetric analogue of a Bresenham line. Inner Z runs along the segment from zero to its length, inner Y points as far toward world up as the direction allows.
- `of(matrix, innerMin, innerMax)`: any invertible affine matrix, including non-uniform scale and shear.

Usage for a penne-like tube:
